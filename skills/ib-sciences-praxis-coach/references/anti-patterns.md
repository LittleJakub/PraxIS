# Anti-Patterns — What Caps Marks (by Criterion)

Comprehensive list of patterns that reliably cap IA marks, drawn from the official assessed samples, examiner reports, the moderation case study, and the 800+ corpus calibration. Use in Mode B (review — identifying what's holding a report back) and Mode C (marking — verifying that a mark isn't being over-credited).

**The principle**: every anti-pattern below has been observed by examiners to cap a criterion at a specific band. If you see it, the mark belongs in the LOWER band — the Hedge Test applies.

## Criterion A — Research Design (/6)

### Caps at 1–2 (strand level: stated, not described)

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| RQ is a broad topic, not a focused question | "How does temperature affect enzymes?" — no quantities, no specific enzyme, no system | Strand 1 at 1–2 |
| RQ answerable by a quick search | "Does pH affect amylase activity?" — the answer is in every textbook | Strand 1 at 1–2 |
| No background theory / context | RQ stated in isolation; no reference to the science behind the variables | Strand 1 at 1–2 |
| IV range/interval unjustified | "I will use 5 concentrations" — no rationale for why these values or this spacing | Strand 2 at 1–2 |
| No trial investigation mentioned | Range chosen by guesswork; no preliminary data | Strand 2 at 1–2 |
| Control variables listed but not controlled | "Temperature will be kept constant" — how? Room temp? Water bath? | Strand 2 at 1–2 |
| Confounding variables not monitored | Room temperature, humidity, light — stated as "controlled" but not recorded | Strand 2 at 1–2 |
| Method is a list of steps with no rationale | "Step 1... Step 2..." — no explanation of WHY each step is done | Strand 3 at 1–2 |
| Method cannot be reproduced | Vague descriptions: "some solution", "appropriate amount", "a while" | Strand 3 at 1–2 |
| Missing safety/ethics/environmental section | Entirely absent — noted explicitly by examiners | Strand 2 penalty |

### Caps at 3–4 (strand level: described, not explained)

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| RQ has context but is generic | "Enzymes are important in biology" — not specific to the investigation | Strand 1 at 3–4 |
| Method is reproducible but not justified | Steps are clear but no rationale for choices (why 5 repeats? why this range?) | Strand 3 at 3–4 |
| Database/simulation work: no screenshots | Screenshots missing or relegated to appendix | Strand 3 at 3–4 |
| Database/simulation work: selection not explained | "I used data from Our World in Data" — no filtering/sampling rationale | Strand 2 at 3–4 |
| Risk assessment is generic | "Wear goggles" without identifying specific hazards | Strand 2 at 3–4 |

### What earns 5–6

- Focused RQ with specific context, named variables, quantities
- Every methodological choice justified in terms of the science
- Trial investigation described and its results used to refine the design
- Database/simulation: screenshots in body, selection/sampling explained
- Safety, ethics, environment addressed specifically

## Criterion B — Data Analysis (/6)

### Caps at 1–2

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| No raw data presented | Only processed values shown | Both strands at 1–2 |
| Units missing or inconsistent | µg/cm³ in one table, µg/m³ in another; seconds vs minutes | Precision strand at 1–2 |
| No uncertainties anywhere | No instrument uncertainties, no error bars, no SD/range | Both strands at 1–2 |
| Data insufficient for processing claimed | 3 data points with a "trend line" | Processing strand at 1–2 |
| Graphs unlabeled | No axis labels, no units, no title | Precision strand at 1–2 |

### Caps at 3–4

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| No significance test (biology) | Claims of difference or correlation without any formal test | Processing strand at 2–3 |
| SD calculated on n < 5 | Penalised, not rewarded — use range instead | Precision strand at 3 |
| Uncertainties present but not interpreted | Error bars on graph but never mentioned in analysis | Processing strand at 3–4 |
| Wrong statistical test | t-test on paired data treated as unpaired; ANOVA on a correlation RQ | Processing strand at 3–4 |
| Outliers removed silently | No justification, no rule stated, no data with/without | Processing strand at 3–4 |
| Connect-the-dots graph instead of best-fit | Shows the data but not the trend | Processing strand at 3–4 |
| Three values claimed as a trend (chemistry) | Explicitly penalised — need ≥5 points for a trend claim | Processing strand at 3 |
| Rate calculated as 1/time | Common chemistry error — rate is not the reciprocal of time | Processing strand at 3 |
| Logarithmic values averaged | Mathematical error — do not average logs | Processing strand at 3 |

### What earns 5–6

- Clear AND precise recording with uncertainties in column headings
- Appropriate processing with justified statistical test (biology) or propagation (physics/chemistry)
- Uncertainties present AND interpreted (error bars identified as SD/SEM, gradient ± uncertainty)
- Outliers identified with a stated rule, data presented with and without
- R² reported for linear fits; gradient interpreted in terms of physical/chemical/biological meaning

## Criterion C — Conclusion (/6)

### Caps at 1–2

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Conclusion does not answer the RQ | Talks about the data but doesn't state what was found | All strands at 1–2 |
| Conclusion contradicts accepted science without noticing | Result disagrees with theory; student doesn't acknowledge | Context strand at 1–2 (hard cap) |
| No comparison to scientific context | Conclusion exists in a vacuum — no literature, no theory | Context strand at 1–2 |

### Caps at 3–4

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Conclusion matches data but doesn't interpret uncertainties | "The mean increased" — but does the uncertainty overlap? | Interpretation strand at 3–4 |
| Comparison to literature is name-dropping | "This agrees with the conservation of momentum" — not a quantitative comparison | Context strand at 3–4 |
| Unreferenced bibliography | Sources listed but never cited in the text | Context strand at 3–4 (physics-specific trap) |
| Conclusion claims causation from correlation | Significant r does not prove cause | Interpretation strand at 3–4 |
| Systematic error mentioned but no direction stated | "There may have been systematic errors" — which way did they shift the result? | Interpretation strand at 3–4 |

### What earns 5–6

- Answer to RQ stated clearly, supported by processed data INCLUDING uncertainties
- Quantitative comparison to accepted values or literature (percentage difference, not just "agrees")
- Systematic errors identified with their DIRECTION explained
- Uncertainties used to justify whether differences are significant or within error

## Criterion D — Evaluation (/6)

### Caps at 1–2

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| No weaknesses identified | "The experiment went well" | All strands at 1–2 |
| Weaknesses listed but no impact explained | "We only did 5 repeats" — so what? How does this affect the conclusion? | Impact strand at 1–2 |
| Improvements are generic | "Take more measurements" — not connected to a specific weakness | Improvements strand at 1–2 |

### Caps at 3–4

| Anti-pattern | Evidence | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Weaknesses identified but impact is vague | "This could have affected the results" — HOW? By how much? In which direction? | Impact strand at 3–4 |
| Improvements not tied to specific weaknesses | Generic list of improvements not connected to the identified problems | Improvements strand at 3–4 |
| Improvements not feasible at school level | Suggests equipment or methods unavailable to the student | Improvements strand at 3–4 |
| "Careless manipulation" claimed as a weakness | Not a valid methodological weakness — examiners explicitly reject this | Impact strand at 3 |
| Only random errors discussed (or only systematic) | Both types should be addressed | Impact strand at 3–4 |

### What earns 5–6

- Specific weaknesses with RELATIVE impact explained (which matters most and why)
- Improvements directly tied to each identified weakness
- Improvements include HOW they would work, not just what they are
- Feasibility awareness — acknowledging what can and cannot be done at school level
- Improvements introduced during the design stage receive credit (chemistry 24/24 pattern)

## The Hedge Test (universal)

If your own justification for a mark contains ANY of these phrases, the criterion belongs in the LOWER band:

- "mostly", "generally", "somewhat", "largely"
- "with potential", "would benefit from", "could be strengthened"
- "not fully", "limited", "adequate but..."
- "shows understanding but..." (the "but" is the signal)
- "attempts to..." (attempting ≠ achieving)

**Real moderation data shows uncalibrated marking runs 2–3 marks per report too generous.** When in doubt between two bands, choose the lower.
