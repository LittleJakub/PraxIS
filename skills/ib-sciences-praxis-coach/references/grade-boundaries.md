# Grade Boundaries — IA Component Context

What the IA marks actually mean in terms of final grades. Use in Mode C (marking — contextualising what a given IA score contributes to the student's final grade) and Mode E (submission — explaining moderation impact to teachers).

**Key fact**: the IA (scientific investigation / practical work) is worth **20% of the final subject grade** across all three sciences (Biology, Chemistry, Physics), at both SL and HL. The remaining 80% comes from external exam papers.

## IA component boundaries (Practical Work out of 24)

These are the **IA component** grade boundaries — the raw mark out of 24 that maps to a component grade. These are distinct from the overall subject grade boundaries.

### Biology (current syllabus)

| Component Grade | Marks out of 24 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 20–24 | Exceptional — strong across all four criteria |
| 6 | 17–19 | Very good — minor gaps in one criterion |
| 5 | 14–16 | Good — solid but with identifiable weaknesses |
| 4 | 11–13 | Adequate — meets basic requirements |
| 3 | 7–10 | Below average — significant gaps |
| 2 | 4–6 | Poor — minimal evidence |
| 1 | 0–3 | Very little or no relevant work |

Source: official session grade-boundaries publications, cross-checked across sessions. Component boundaries are stable across sessions — the IA component grade 7 threshold has been 20/24 consistently since the current syllabus was introduced.

## What this means for IA marking

1. **A score of 18/24 (component grade 6) is a strong IA**. It gives the student a solid foundation — they need roughly 60–65% on their exam papers to reach an overall grade 6 or 7.

2. **A score of 14/24 (component grade 5) is the median**. Most students land here. It means the exam papers need to do more work — roughly 70%+ on papers to reach an overall grade 6.

3. **A score of 20+/24 (component grade 7) is rare and powerful**. It effectively gives the student a 4–5 mark head start toward the overall grade 7 boundary. Even mediocre exam performance can still yield an overall 6.

4. **A score below 11/24 (below component grade 4) is a liability**. The student needs very strong exam performance to compensate.

5. **Moderation adjusts marks, not grades**. If a school's internal marks are consistently generous, the moderator will pull the whole cohort down. The moderation case study in `marking-calibration.md` shows a school losing 19 marks across 6 reports — that's an average of 3.2 marks per report. Accurate internal marking protects against moderation shock.
