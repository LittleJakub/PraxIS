# Physics Sample Patterns — the Official Assessed Samples

Author's own-words distillation of the examiner commentary from the official Physics assessed samples (5 available: 11/24, 19/24, 16/24, 17/24, and a **24/24**). The criteria descriptors are word-for-word identical across the sciences; the patterns below are the subject-specific ways examiners apply them. Use alongside `sample-patterns-biology.md` (biology) and `marking-calibration.md` (quantified band behaviour).

## What the 24/24 looks like (simple harmonic motion: period vs √mass)

- **RD**: context = the known theory the investigation confirms; a long procedure was judged excessive in count but every step relevant; safety addressed even when trivial; **top marks do not mean perfection** — they represent a high level of achievement for candidates at DP level.
- **DA**: a formal approach to error analysis that follows the required skills closely; separate graphs for best-fit and for max/min gradients (a clarity bonus); basic arithmetic (averages, spreadsheet square-roots) need not be displayed.
- **C**: conclusion justified through the analysis *including* uncertainties; when the aim is a functional relationship, the accepted context is the known equation — no literature value needed, because the research aim concerned the proportional function itself, not a numerical constant.
- **E**: random AND systematic errors both addressed; insightful comments on the *cause* of the systematic shift (unaccounted additional mass); assumptions of the model addressed; improvements focused and direct.

## Mark-moving patterns (physics-specific)

1. **Confirming a known theory is the classic physics IA** — but RD 5–6 needs more than the law itself: presenting Hooke's law and stopping there was judged a broad context at best, with more sound physics required (Phys 03, RD 3–4). The context must go beyond the syllabus law.
2. **The context strand of Conclusion is a trap**: an unreferenced bibliography caps it — a sample listed sources in the bibliography but never cited any of them in the text (Phys 03, strand 2 near zero). And **name-dropping is not comparison**: repeatedly quoting the conservation of momentum and Newton's laws does not constitute a justification (Phys 01).
3. **Graphs are the physics evidence**: inappropriate best-fit curves, no thought to the y-axis intercept (Phys 01) and missing systematic-shift annotation (Phys 06) all cap Data analysis; separate best-fit and max/min-gradient graphs are praised (Phys 07).
4. **Uncertainty bars and gradient ranges are inspected individually** — plotting different y-uncertainty bar values is unusual but praised; a gradient range that is too wide is penalized (Phys 02).
5. **Ambitious designs are judged, not rewarded** — two surfaces when one suffices was judged equivalent to performing two investigations (Phys 06); a hands-on comparison where a simulation would be more appropriate is noted. Scope creep without comparison value caps RD.
6. **Evaluation should assess the overall method, not internals** — comments should address the overall approach to obtaining and analysing the data, not the internals of the simulation software (Phys 06).
7. **Qualitative hypotheses and repeated basic theory read as padding** — a candidate who wrote too much basic Newtonian physics without getting specific about the actual system under study (Phys 01); the hypothesis should be quantitative where the RQ is quantitative.
8. **A basic investigation must still produce detailed evaluation** — the simpler the investigation, the more room there is for detailed, specific comments on method, procedure, and limitations (Phys 02) — simplicity of design does not excuse a thin Evaluation.
