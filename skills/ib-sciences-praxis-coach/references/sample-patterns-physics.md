# Physics Sample Patterns — the Official Assessed Samples

Author's own-words distillation of the examiner commentary from the official Physics assessed samples (all 7 available: 11/24, 19/24, 16/24, 15/24, 21/24, 17/24, and a **24/24**). The criteria descriptors are word-for-word identical across the sciences; the patterns below are the subject-specific ways examiners apply them. Use alongside `sample-patterns-biology.md` (biology) and `marking-calibration.md` (quantified band behaviour).

## What the 24/24 looks like (simple harmonic motion: period vs √mass)

- **RD**: context = the known theory the investigation confirms; a long procedure was judged excessive in count but every step relevant; safety addressed even when trivial; **a top mark is not perfection** — it signals a high standard of work for a Diploma-level student.
- **DA**: a rigorous error-analysis method that tracks the required skills; separate graphs for best-fit and for max/min gradients (a clarity bonus); basic arithmetic (averages, spreadsheet square-roots) need not be displayed.
- **C**: conclusion justified through the analysis *including* uncertainties; when the aim is a functional relationship, the accepted context is the known equation — no literature value needed, because the aim was the proportional relationship itself, not a numerical constant.
- **E**: random AND systematic errors both addressed; insightful comments on the *cause* of the systematic shift (unaccounted additional mass); assumptions of the model addressed; improvements focused and direct.

## Mark-moving patterns (physics-specific)

1. **Confirming a known theory is the classic physics IA** — but RD 5–6 needs more than the law itself: presenting Hooke's law and stopping there was judged a broad context at best, with more sound physics required (Phys 03, RD 3–4). The context must go beyond the syllabus law.
2. **The context strand of Conclusion is a trap**: an unreferenced bibliography caps it — a sample listed sources in the bibliography but never cited any of them in the text (Phys 03, strand 2 near zero). And **name-dropping is not comparison**: repeatedly quoting the conservation of momentum and Newton's laws is not itself an argument — naming a law is not the same as checking the result against it (Phys 01).
3. **Graphs are the physics evidence**: inappropriate best-fit curves, no thought to the y-axis intercept (Phys 01) and missing systematic-shift annotation (Phys 06) all cap Data analysis; separate best-fit and max/min-gradient graphs are praised (Phys 07).
4. **Uncertainty bars and gradient ranges are inspected individually** — plotting different y-uncertainty bar values is unusual but praised; a gradient range that is too wide is penalized (Phys 02).
5. **Ambitious designs are judged, not rewarded** — two surfaces when one suffices was judged equivalent to performing two investigations (Phys 06); a hands-on comparison where a simulation would be more appropriate is noted. Scope creep without comparison value caps RD.
6. **Evaluation should assess the overall method, not internals** — comments should address the overall approach to obtaining and analysing the data, not the internals of the simulation software (Phys 06).
7. **Qualitative hypotheses and repeated basic theory read as padding** — a candidate who spent pages restating elementary mechanics without ever getting specific about the actual system under study (Phys 01); the hypothesis should be quantitative where the RQ is quantitative.
8. **A basic investigation must still produce detailed evaluation** — the simpler the investigation, the more room there is for detailed, specific comments on method, procedure, and limitations (Phys 02) — simplicity of design does not excuse a thin Evaluation.
9. **Context padding caps Research design** — Phys 04 (15/24, supernovae database IA): six pages of general astrophysics history that never connected to what the candidate planned to do; the examiner noted the context could have been condensed to a single page. Repetition of textbook material fails the criterion's communication strand (3–4, not 5–6).
10. **Least-count-only uncertainties are not enough in a rich context** — Phys 04: uncertainties recognized only at instrument resolution, when the astrophysics setting demanded treatment of data selection and systematic effects; sample calculations and processing steps were also missing (DA 3–4). A database IA must show how the data became the plotted quantity.
11. **Evaluation must be about *your* data selection, not the field's** — Phys 04: the candidate offered general astrophysical caveats but never connected them to their own investigation; the examiner expected an appreciation of the strengths and limitations of the chosen dataset (the type-1a supernovae selection) — and explicitly does not expect original solutions to open research problems like gravitational lensing or interstellar dust. Generic field-level caveats = E 1–2.
12. **Magnitude-awareness in the conclusion is rewarded** — Phys 04: landing roughly a billion years out in the age of the universe was explicitly acceptable given the processing; what cost the sixth mark was missing a genuine appreciation of the accepted Hubble value. Conclusions should locate their result against the accepted value with honest scale, not fake precision.
13. **Pre-testing is a top-band signal in RD** — Phys 05 (21/24, a falling-object drag-coefficient investigation): an impressive pre-testing section, scope and limits addressed early, standard high-school experiment done with focus → RD top band even with a few details left for clarification.
14. **The context comparison can be shape-level — Wikipedia is acceptable** — Phys 05: no published value exists for such an object's drag coefficient; comparing the general shape of the result (and the coefficient's order of magnitude) against accepted physics was sufficient — and a Wikipedia source was accepted. Conclusions compare what the experiment can legitimately claim.
15. **An improvement that cannot fix the identified weakness earns nothing** — Phys 05: proposing a higher frame rate to improve a scatter that was already high-precision — the examiner could not see how it would help, and the weakness's impact on the conclusion was never spelled out (E 4). Improvements must demonstrably address a weakness that actually mattered.
