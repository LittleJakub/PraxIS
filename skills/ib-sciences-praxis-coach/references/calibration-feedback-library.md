# Feedback & Justification Library — Paraphrased Examiner Language

*Extracted 28 August 2026. The fine-grained feedback layer built from the corpus evaluator commentary (weaknesses and strengths). Every entry is a paraphrased, reusable phrasing for a specific issue an agent will see while reviewing a report — paired with the data-driven band evidence (how reports carrying this issue actually score). Use in Mode B (feedback generation) and Mode C (justifying a mark). No verbatim commentary; aggregates only.*

## Research Design

### What is weak (feedback for improvement)

**IV range or intervals set without justification** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: The independent variable's range is chosen but the report never explains why that span, or why those spacings, were selected.
- Teacher feedback: Why these concentrations and not others? Add one sentence tying your chosen range to what you expect the relationship to do.
- Student note: State the reason for the range and spacing of your independent variable before you collect data.
- Band: Research design strand 2 at 3-4

**Control variables listed but not explained how held/monitored** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 46 of 596 reports (8%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.5** vs **4.7** without it (Δ -0.2).
- Examiner comment: Controls are named but there is no statement of how each was held steady, or how the ones that could not be held were monitored.
- Teacher feedback: For each control, say how you kept it constant — or, if you couldn't, how you measured and recorded it.
- Student note: List your controls, then for each write the method used to keep it steady (or monitor it).
- Band: Research design strand 2 at 3-4

**Background too general, not focused on the RQ** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 10 of 596 reports (2%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.1** vs **4.7** without it (Δ -0.6).
- Examiner comment: The background surveys the topic broadly rather than building the specific case for this investigation.
- Teacher feedback: Cut the general topic summary. Keep only the theory that directly explains why your IV should affect your DV.
- Student note: A general overview will not reach the top band — make the background argue for your specific question.
- Band: Research design strand 1 at 3-4

**Safety/ethics/environment not addressed (or only named)** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 65 of 596 reports (11%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.7** vs **4.6** without it (Δ +0.1).
- Examiner comment: Safety, ethical or environmental issues are either absent or merely named without the mitigations that handle them.
- Teacher feedback: For each hazard: who is exposed, how you reduce it, and what you do if it goes wrong. If none exist, say so.
- Student note: Naming a risk is not addressing it — add the control measures, and state explicitly when there are none.
- Band: Research design strand 2 penalty

**Repeat count not justified against the planned processing** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 19 of 596 reports (3%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.6** vs **4.6** without it (Δ -0.0).
- Examiner comment: The number of repeats is stated but not linked to the processing planned — five repeats only matter if a standard deviation or trend is the aim.
- Teacher feedback: Say why this many repeats: are you fitting a trend, averaging, or testing significance? Match the count to the test.
- Student note: Justify your replicate number against the statistics you intend to use.
- Band: Research design strand 2 at 3

**No trial investigation described** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 6 of 596 reports (1%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **5.0** vs **4.6** without it (Δ +0.4).
- Examiner comment: There is no evidence of a trial run to refine the method and set the sampling.
- Teacher feedback: Run the method once first and describe what you changed as a result — this is a cheap, reliable top-band signal.
- Student note: Describe your trial run and how its results changed your final design.
- Band: Research design strand 3 at 3-4

**Method not reproducible (units/quantities/calibration unclear)** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 154 of 596 reports (26%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.6** vs **4.7** without it (Δ -0.1).
- Examiner comment: The procedure cannot be reproduced as written — quantities, units, or calibration steps are missing or ambiguous.
- Teacher feedback: Could another student follow this exactly? Add the volumes, timings and settings that are currently implied.
- Student note: Write the procedure so someone else could repeat it without asking you anything.
- Band: Research design strand 3 at 3-4

**Database/simulation source or sampling not explained** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 62 of 596 reports (10%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.6** vs **4.7** without it (Δ -0.0).
- Examiner comment: The data source is named but the choice, reliability, and the way data were sampled or filtered are not explained.
- Teacher feedback: For a database or simulation: why this source, is it reliable, and exactly how did you select or filter the data?
- Student note: Show screenshots with URLs, and explain your selection/sampling of the data.
- Band: Research design strand 2 at 3-4

- Student note: Your justifications are strong; keep tying each choice to the scientific reason behind it.

**Trial run described and used to refine the design** — *all subjects*

- Student note: Your trial section is strong; make sure the reader sees what you changed because of it.
- Band: Research design strand 3 at 5-6

**Safety/ethics addressed specifically with mitigations** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 69 of 596 reports (12%) carry this; those reports average Research Design **4.8** vs **4.6** without it (Δ +0.2).
- Examiner comment: Safety and ethics are handled specifically, with mitigations rather than mere awareness.
- Teacher feedback: The risk assessment has real control measures — this is what the strand rewards.
- Student note: You named hazards and their mitigations — keep that standard across every section.
- Band: Research design strand 2 at 5-6

## Data Analysis

### What is weak (feedback for improvement)

**Uncertainties omitted for one or more quantities** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 594 of 596 reports (100%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.8** vs **4.0** without it (Δ -0.2).
- Examiner comment: Uncertainties are missing for key measured or derived quantities, so the precision of the data cannot be judged.
- Teacher feedback: Every measured and derived value needs a ±. Add the ones that are missing — column headings are the tidy place.
- Student note: Record the uncertainty for each quantity, not just the ones that were easy.
- Band: Data analysis ≤ 3-4

**Uncertainties not propagated through processing** — *chemistry · physics*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: The raw uncertainties are given but never carried through the calculations to the processed values.
- Teacher feedback: Show what happens to ± when you average, divide, or take a gradient — each derived number needs its own uncertainty.
- Student note: Propagate: add/subtract uses absolute, multiply/divide uses percentage — and exponents only at HL.
- Band: Data analysis uncertainty strand at 3

**Propagation performed with the wrong operation** — *chemistry · physics*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: Propagation is attempted but the wrong combination rule is used (absolute where percentage is required, or vice versa).
- Teacher feedback: Check the rule: sums use absolute uncertainties, products and quotients use percentage uncertainties.
- Student note: Match the propagation rule to the arithmetic — it's not one-size-fits-all.
- Band: Data analysis uncertainty strand at 3-4

**Uncertainties unrealistically constant / not discussed** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 22 of 596 reports (4%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.9** vs **3.8** without it (Δ +0.1).
- Examiner comment: Uncertainties are either carried as a single constant across unlike quantities, or never discussed against the results.
- Teacher feedback: Each value should carry its own uncertainty, and somewhere you should say what the ± means for your conclusion.
- Student note: Don't reuse one ± for everything; and interpret what your uncertainties imply.
- Band: Data analysis uncertainty strand at 3-4

**Table/graph formatting flaws (headings, units, labels)** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 203 of 596 reports (34%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.6** vs **3.9** without it (Δ -0.3).
- Examiner comment: Tables and graphs carry misaligned headings, missing units, or absent labels, so the data cannot be read cleanly.
- Teacher feedback: Units and uncertainties belong in the column headings; every graph needs labelled axes with units and a title.
- Student note: Tidy tables and labelled graphs are free marks — check headings, units and axis labels.
- Band: Data analysis communication strand at 3

**No error bars where they would clarify the trend** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 187 of 596 reports (31%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.5** vs **3.9** without it (Δ -0.4).
- Examiner comment: Error bars are absent, so the reliability of the trend line cannot be assessed.
- Teacher feedback: Add error bars (say whether they are SD, SEM, or range) — they are one of the sharpest markers of a strong analysis.
- Student note: Plot error bars and label which measure of spread they show.
- Band: Data analysis at 3-4

**Statistical test missing, wrong, or unjustified** — *biology*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: A difference or correlation is claimed but no significance test is run, or the test does not match the question.
- Teacher feedback: For a comparison or correlation, run the matching test and report its statistic and p-value.
- Student note: State the null hypothesis, name the test and the software, and report the p-value.
- Band: Data analysis processing strand at 2-3

**Significant figures / decimal places inconsistent** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 287 of 596 reports (48%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.7** vs **3.9** without it (Δ -0.2).
- Examiner comment: Significant figures or decimal places are inconsistent between the data and the processed values.
- Teacher feedback: Match decimal places to the measurement precision, and keep them consistent through every table.
- Student note: Keep the same decimal places throughout; don't let the calculator set your precision.
- Band: Data analysis communication strand at 3

**Processing incomplete or key steps not shown** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 130 of 596 reports (22%) carry this; those reports average Data Analysis **3.7** vs **3.8** without it (Δ -0.1).
- Examiner comment: The processing is incomplete — derived values appear without the working, or the raw data is plotted where processed data belongs.
- Teacher feedback: Show one full worked calculation for each type of processing; don't just present the final numbers.
- Student note: Include a worked example of your calculations, not only the results.
- Band: Data analysis processing strand at 3

**Rate or derived quantity miscalculated (rate ≠ 1/time)** — *chemistry*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: A derived quantity is miscalculated — for example, rate taken as the reciprocal of time, or logarithms averaged.
- Teacher feedback: Rate is not 1/time; do not average logarithms. Recompute the derived quantities correctly.
- Student note: Check the definition of every derived quantity before you compute it.
- Band: Data analysis processing strand at 2-3

## Conclusion

### What is weak (feedback for improvement)

**No numeric comparison against known values** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 484 of 596 reports (81%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **3.6** vs **4.3** without it (Δ -0.7).
- Examiner comment: The conclusion never checks the result against an accepted value or a published figure.
- Teacher feedback: Find the accepted value for what you measured and compare — as a percentage difference, not just 'close to the literature'.
- Student note: A named comparison beats a vague one; give the numbers.
- Band: Conclusion strand 2 at 3-4 (with no comparison at all, ≤ 3)

**Uncertainties not used to qualify the conclusion** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 194 of 596 reports (33%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **3.7** vs **3.8** without it (Δ -0.1).
- Examiner comment: The conclusion ignores the uncertainties — whether the result is distinguishable from a null result is never discussed.
- Teacher feedback: Say what your ± does to the answer: do the error bars overlap? Is the difference real or within noise?
- Student note: Use your uncertainties to decide whether the effect is real before you claim it.
- Band: Conclusion strand 1 at 3-4

**Conclusion claims more than the data support** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 89 of 596 reports (15%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **3.7** vs **3.8** without it (Δ -0.1).
- Examiner comment: The conclusion reaches beyond the evidence — correlation is presented as causation, or a weak trend as proof.
- Teacher feedback: Softly: state what the data actually show, and where the limits are. Correlation is not cause.
- Student note: Match the strength of your claim to the strength of your evidence.
- Band: Conclusion strand 1 at 3-4

**Conclusion does not answer the research question** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 7 of 596 reports (1%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **3.4** vs **3.8** without it (Δ -0.3).
- Examiner comment: The conclusion describes the data but never states the answer to the research question.
- Teacher feedback: Open the conclusion with a direct answer to your research question, then support it.
- Student note: Say explicitly what your question's answer is — don't leave the reader to infer it.
- Band: Conclusion strand 1 at 2-3

### What is praised (feedback for reinforcement)

**Conclusion justified through the analysis including uncertainties** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 344 of 596 reports (58%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **4.2** vs **3.2** without it (Δ +1.0).
- Examiner comment: The conclusion is argued through the processed data and its uncertainties, not merely asserted.
- Teacher feedback: The conclusion is argued from the processed data, uncertainties included — exactly what strand 1 rewards.
- Student note: Your conclusion is well-supported; keep the justification, not just the claim.
- Band: Conclusion strand 1 at 5-6

**Numeric comparison against known values** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 416 of 596 reports (70%) carry this; those reports average Conclusion **4.0** vs **3.3** without it (Δ +0.6).
- Examiner comment: The result is compared quantitatively to accepted values or published material.
- Teacher feedback: A genuine numerical comparison to the literature — this is the strand-2 differentiator.
- Student note: Your comparison to the accepted value is strong; keep the numbers explicit.
- Band: Conclusion strand 2 at 5-6

## Evaluation

### What is weak (feedback for improvement)

**Weakness impact on the results never explained/ranked** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 492 of 596 reports (83%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **3.5** vs **4.6** without it (Δ -1.1).
- Examiner comment: Weaknesses are listed but their effect on the results is never explained or ranked — the single most common gap.
- Teacher feedback: For each weakness: how much and in which direction did it shift the results? Rank them by importance.
- Student note: Don't just list problems — say what each one did to your results and which mattered most.
- Band: Evaluation ≤ 4

**Improvements generic or not tied to the weaknesses** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 27 of 596 reports (5%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **2.7** vs **3.7** without it (Δ -1.1).
- Examiner comment: The improvements are generic ('more trials', 'better equipment') and not connected to the specific weaknesses identified.
- Teacher feedback: Each improvement should fix a named weakness, and say how it would work.
- Student note: Tie every improvement to the specific weakness it addresses.
- Band: Evaluation improvements strand at 3-4

**Improvement cannot fix the identified weakness** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 100 of 596 reports (17%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **3.9** vs **3.6** without it (Δ +0.3).
- Examiner comment: The proposed improvement could not actually remedy the weakness it names, or is not feasible at school level.
- Teacher feedback: Check each improvement actually fixes the problem, and that a student could do it with school equipment.
- Student note: An improvement that can't fix the weakness earns nothing — make sure yours targets the real issue.
- Band: Evaluation improvements strand at 3-4

**Systematic vs random error not distinguished (or no direction)** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 34 of 596 reports (6%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **3.8** vs **3.7** without it (Δ +0.1).
- Examiner comment: Systematic and random errors are never told apart, or a systematic error is named without saying which way it moved the result.
- Teacher feedback: Separate systematic from random, and for each systematic error say which way it moved the result.
- Student note: A systematic error needs a direction — say whether it raised or lowered your result.
- Band: Evaluation at 3-4

**Limitations that the design stage should have caught** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: small sample (not computed).
- Examiner comment: The limitations listed are ones that should have been resolved during the design — these do not earn credit at higher bands.
- Teacher feedback: The best evaluations discuss weaknesses that only surfaced during the work, not ones you could have fixed earlier.
- Student note: Save your evaluation for what you could not have anticipated, and show the fixes you introduced mid-investigation.
- Band: Evaluation at 3-4

### What is praised (feedback for reinforcement)

**Weaknesses ranked by impact with direction explained** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 316 of 596 reports (53%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **4.2** vs **3.0** without it (Δ +1.2).
- Examiner comment: Weaknesses are ranked by their effect on the results, with the direction of each shift explained.
- Teacher feedback: The evaluation ranks weaknesses by impact and explains the direction of the error — the hardest criterion done well.
- Student note: Your evaluation has what most miss: the ranking and direction of each weakness.
- Band: Evaluation at 5-6

**Improvements tied to specific weaknesses and feasible** — *all subjects*

- Frequency: 593 of 596 reports (99%) carry this; those reports average Evaluation **3.7** vs **0.7** without it (Δ +3.0).
- Examiner comment: Each improvement is tied to a specific weakness, explained, and feasible in a school setting.
- Teacher feedback: The improvements are specific, tied to real weaknesses, and doable — strong evaluation.
- Student note: Your improvements match your weaknesses well; add how each would work if you want the top band.
- Band: Evaluation improvements strand at 5-6
