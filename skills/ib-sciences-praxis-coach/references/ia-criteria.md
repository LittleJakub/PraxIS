# IA Criteria Digest — IB DP Sciences 

Teacher-authored digest in our own words, distilled from the official Biology guide and TSM — **the four-criteria IA model is identical across Biology, Chemistry, and Physics** (Research design / Data analysis / Conclusion / Evaluation, 6 marks each, 24 total; the Chemistry and Physics guides carry the same descriptors). Facts and structure; paraphrase, not reproduction. **The official guide remains the authority for exact descriptor wording** — when marking, cross-check against the teacher's copy if available.

## Assessment structure (facts)

- One task: the **scientific investigation**. 10 hours of teaching time, 20% of final grade, same at SL and HL.
- Report max **3,000 words**. Excluded from the count: charts/diagrams, data tables, equations/formulas/calculations, citations/references, bibliography, headers.
- Report starts with: title, candidate code, group member codes (if applicable), word count. No cover page or contents page required.
- **Four criteria, 6 marks each, total 24**: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion, Evaluation.
- Marking: best-fit against level descriptors; whole numbers only; mark positively; criteria judged independently (no assumed mark distribution).
- Approaches allowed: hands-on lab work, fieldwork, spreadsheet analysis/modelling, database extraction, simulation.
- Every student has a unique research question; no two students present the same raw data.
- Collaborative work optional, groups ≤ 3: different IV, or same IV with different DV, or different data from a communal set; **all authoring is individual** (methodology description included). A school-built class database counts as a database investigation (methodology = how data was filtered/sampled).
- Referencing expected; omitted/improper referencing = academic malpractice. Broad facts need no citation; specific facts (e.g. "alpha-amylases have an optimum pH of 6.8") do.

## Research design — 6 marks

Assesses how well the report communicates the methodology (purpose and practice) used to address the research question.

The three strands, by markband (1–2 → 3–4 → 5–6):

1. **Research question with context**: stated without context → outlined in a broad context → described in a specific and appropriate context. Context = reference to the DV and IV (or two correlated variables), a concise description of the system the question is embedded in, and directly relevant background theory.
2. **Methodological considerations**: stated → described → explained. Covers: how IV/DV are measured; for databases/models, the choice of source and the sampling of data; scope/quantity/quality decisions (range, interval or frequency of the IV, repetition, precision); control variables and how they are controlled; safety, ethical and environmental issues taken into account.
3. **Methodology description**: must let the investigation be reproduced — specific materials, precise procedural steps, no unnecessary or repetitive information.

Teacher-check notes (from the TSM): RQ unique; IV present (or two correlated variables); DV or derived DV present; variables quantifiable; scientific name where relevant; background focused on the exact RQ (a general overview never reaches the top strand); protocol records the method *as used* including trialling (not a proposed plan); standard protocols cited; sampling rate determined by trialling; confounding variables identified as *monitored* when not controllable; a trial investigation described; risk assessment with mitigations (stating an issue ≠ addressing it); if no safety/ethics/environmental concerns exist, the student says so explicitly; fieldwork includes a site description; database work includes screenshots with URLs and an explained filtering/sampling procedure.

## Data analysis — 6 marks

Assesses evidence that data were recorded, processed and presented in ways relevant to the research question. (Interpreting the data in relation to the RQ belongs to Conclusion.)

1. **Communication of recording and processing**: neither clear nor precise → either clear or precise → both clear and precise. Clear = method of processing easily understood; precise = conventions followed (graph/table annotation, units, decimal places, significant figures).
2. **Consideration of uncertainties**: limited evidence → evidence with some significant omissions/inaccuracies → appropriate consideration.
3. **Processing relevant to the RQ**: with major omissions/inaccuracies/inconsistencies → with some significant omissions → appropriately and accurately. (Major issues impede a valid conclusion; significant issues limit validity or detail.)

Key conventions (TSM): SI or metric units only (°F, cups, inches not acceptable); consistent decimal places matched to precision (significant-figure conventions not required, but correct if used); means/SD/ranges at the end of a column are sufficient evidence of processing; for software, screenshots with formulas acceptable; statistical tests require a null and alternative hypothesis stated (not necessarily at the start) and the software source named; raw and processed data may share a table; representative samples of large datasets allowed; device-derived values (e.g. an instrument that outputs "rate") are raw data; graphs from software are raw data; gradient/area-under-curve may extend processing.

## Conclusion — 6 marks

Assesses how successfully the student answers the research question with regard to the analysis and the accepted scientific context.

1. **Answering the RQ**: conclusion stated but not supported by the analysis → described but not fully consistent with the analysis → justified and fully consistent with the analysis. Full consistency requires **interpreting processed data including associated uncertainties**.
2. **Scientific context**: superficial comparison → some relevant comparison → justified through relevant comparison. Context = published material, published values, course notes, textbooks; citations traceable.

Teacher-check notes: explain trends validly; address the RQ in its context; no bias in interpretation (do not force a conclusion the data don't support); interpret statistical tests (p < 0.05 is the conventional significance threshold); discuss the impact of uncertainties and the reliability of the data; acknowledge sample-size limits; use terms like "optima", "maxima", "intercepts" where relevant; inconclusive data are acceptable if discussed honestly; where no accepted value exists (e.g. some ecology studies), argue whether the result is reasonable.

## Evaluation — 6 marks

Assesses evidence of evaluating the methodology and suggesting improvements.

1. **Weaknesses/limitations**: generic weaknesses stated → specific weaknesses/limitations described → relative impact of specific weaknesses/limitations **explained**. Generic = true of any methodology, not this one. Weaknesses may concern control of variables, precision of measurement, or variation in the data; limitations concern scope (range of data, confines of the system, applicability of assumptions).
2. **Improvements**: realistic improvements stated → described and relevant to the identified weaknesses → explained and tied to those weaknesses. Improvements must be feasible in a school/field environment; "take more measurements" or "use a more precise method" count only when connected to a specific issue.

Teacher-check notes: weaknesses exclude careless-manipulation errors and hypothetical events with no evidence; an experiment always has limitations even with perfect conditions (a simulation may have few weaknesses but real limitations); judge impact qualitatively — propagation of uncertainties is not expected; systematic errors (uncalibrated instruments, human error) affect accuracy, random variation affects precision; measuring can change the system (cold thermometer cools the sample; an observer alters animal behaviour); for databases, do not re-validate sources (done in Research design) but reflection on data curation adds value.

## Command terms in the descriptors (depth ladder)

AO1: state. AO2: identify, outline, describe. AO3: explain, justify. Higher bands demand the deeper terms.

## Tools and skills relevant to investigations

- **Tool 1 — Experimental techniques**: safety; measuring mass/volume/time/temperature/length; observations (counts, annotated diagrams, qualitative notes, classifying); chromatography, colorimetry/spectrophotometry, serial dilutions, microscopy + eyepiece graticule, temporary mounts, identification/classification, random and systematic sampling, karyograms, cladogram analysis.
- **Tool 2 — Technology**: sensors; databases; models/simulations; spreadsheets; graphical representation; computer modelling; image analysis.
- **Tool 3 — Mathematics**: mean/median/mode; range, SD, SE, IQR; rates of change; percentage change/difference; chi-squared, t-test, Simpson reciprocal index, Lincoln index; uncertainties recorded as ranges (±); error bars from ranges/SD/SE; R² for trend-line fit; correlation coefficient r.
- **Inquiry process**: exploring & designing → collecting & processing data → concluding & evaluating.

## Legacy note — the older syllabus (older cohorts)

The older-syllabus scheme had **five** criteria: Personal engagement (2), Exploration (6), Analysis (6), Evaluation (6), Communication (4) — total 24. Personal engagement rewarded the student's interest/initiative; Exploration covered design (RQ, variables, methodology, safety); Analysis covered data processing and presentation; Evaluation covered conclusion + evaluation (combined); Communication covered structure and clarity. Confirm the cohort before marking; the current four-criterion scheme is this skill's default.
