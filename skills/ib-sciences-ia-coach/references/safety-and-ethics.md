# Safety & Ethics Digest (IB sciences experimentation guidelines + lab safety)

Teacher-authored digest in our own words. Applies to all IB science experiments — assessed or not, in the lab or the field. Local/national regulations always override and are the minimum standard.

**Subject specifics**: the core principles below are shared. Biology-specific treatment (microbiology, human participants, animals, fieldwork) is collected in `biology-conventions.md`; chemistry hazard classes and physics electrical/radiation/laser/mechanical hazards with their control measures are covered in `chemistry-conventions.md` and `physics-conventions.md` — consult the relevant one in Mode A/D when the subject is named.

## Core principles

- Purpose of school experimentation: advance the student's learning, not generate new scientific knowledge. **No one is put at risk; no harm to other life forms.**
- The **3 Rs** for animal use: **replacement** (cells, plants, simulations), **refinement** (reduce harm/distress), **reduction** (fewest animals).
- Students must not work in a laboratory unsupervised. Teachers consult safety information (e.g. CLEAPSS, ACS Institute).

## Risk assessment — the four steps

1. Identify the hazards (equipment, field conditions, health conditions, substances, energy sources, environment, organisms).
2. Recognize and assess the potential risks.
3. Minimize risks with appropriate, effective control measures.
4. Prepare for emergencies.

A risk assessment is required **before** any experiment, lab or field. Hazard categories and examples: object (scalpel → cuts → cut away from body), substance (hydrogen peroxide → burns/eye damage → PPE), energy (UV source → eye damage → eye protection + not viewing directly), terrain (slippery rocks → falls → grip footwear), organism (microbe → infection → aseptic protocol).

## Animals

- Only observation/measurement of **natural behaviour** within the animal's natural environment range; no compromise to health; no drugs/medicines; no diet/environment manipulation beyond natural tolerance; no deliberate euthanasia for experiments; anything removed from habitat returned unharmed promptly.
- Dissection: never required; opt-out always allowed; reduce numbers; prefer simulations or ethically sourced animal tissue; **no live organisms**; ethical sources only; no roadkill.

## Humans (physiology/behaviour experiments)

- Supervised-teacher approval required; **written consent forms mandatory** (informed, confidential); under-16s need parental/guardian consent.
- Moderate–vigorous physical activity requires a health declaration (e.g. PAR-Q).
- **Not acceptable**: administering alcohol, drugs, medicines, dietary supplements, caffeine, energy drinks; any body fluids (sweat, blood, urine, saliva).
- Consent forms are the one permitted appendix in the IA report.

## Microbiology

- **No body fluids; no raw milk.** Non-pathogenic cultures only, from reputable specialist suppliers.
- Incubate at ≤ 25 °C; aerobic (containers not fully sealed, edges not taped shut); never reopen sealed containers; standard aseptic technique; no environmental or hand-swab culturing; **no antibiotic-resistance testing**; disposal per local rules.

## Fieldwork

- Minimal impact; return habitats to original condition; avoid protected/endangered species areas and sites of special scientific interest; identify and count species in situ, return removed specimens safely.
- Site risk assessment before visiting: terrain, water currents, weather/tides, hazardous organisms (ticks, venomous plants/animals); appropriate clothing/footwear; contingency plans; procedures for groups away from supervision.

## Laboratory infrastructure (from the IB lab-safety document)

- Essential: trained staff, displayed safety rules, splash-resistant spectacles + protective gloves for chemicals, fire extinguishers, fire blanket where open flames used, fume hood where appropriate, eyewash station, first aid kit; emergency shower where appropriate; labelled chemical containers; **a maintained chemical inventory**; proper chemical storage and disposal; earth-leakage/overload protection; independent gas/electricity cut-offs.
- Desirable: dedicated subject labs, adequate space (~1 sink per 4 students), annual electrical inspection, apparatus inventory with age and last safety-check date.

## How this shows up in the IA

- Research design: safety, ethical, and environmental issues must be *taken into account* — with mitigations, not just awareness. If none apply, the student states so explicitly.
- Human subjects: evidence of consent forms expected. A consent form is good ethics but is **not** evidence that safety was considered.
- Databases/simulations: safety/environment mostly N/A, but ethical issues around data use are to the student's credit if raised.
- Examiners refer severe safety risks to the IB, and the school may be contacted.
